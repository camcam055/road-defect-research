6.0 Conclusion

This project has been successfully developed and evaluated an AI-based road surface detection system to investigate its potential application for New Zealand pavement conditions. A baseline model was first trained using the RDD2022 dataset which included defects in an international setting. The labels for this dataset where then converted into YOLO text format for training and used to train multiple YOLO models. Then New Zealand specific road defect footage was then collected and used to train the model on New Zealand pavement conditions.

The preliminary results showed that the model can learn pavement defects from the international dataset. Testing between the small, large and extra-large YOLO models showed that increasing the model size led improved performance up to a point. The larger model achieved the best balance between performance and accuracy, over the small and extra-large model. However, when testing the extra-large model, the model did not improve compared to the large model. This may suggest that increasing the model while keeping the image quality and the dataset the same may not mean that the model will improve. So, the final model that is selected should be judged on a large New Zealand specific dataset and real-world performance rather than performance using training results.

Following onto the preliminary results, further training was carried out to improve the YOLO model’s ability to detect pavement defects on New Zealand specific pavement conditions and environment. The final result showed that the model was able to identify different types of defects across different types of conditions such as pavement conditions and environmental conditions. However, the detection accuracy, reliability and consistency remained a limitation. The real-world testing showed that the AI would perform better than what the validation results in training would show, however this was based on a visual observation and was not measured to confirm this. The result highlighted the importance of using high-quality images, large and varied database to achieve consistent defect detection across different road conditions.

In addition to training the YOLO model. A Python and workflow were developed to allow users to upload road footage. Automatically extract frames and GPS coordinates, run the YOLO model to detect and classify pavement defects, display the results on an interactive map, and create a small PDF file listing all the defects and location. The workflow was built to simplify the process of analysing road footage and reviewing detected defects. A simplified Pavement Distress Index (PDI) was also developed to provide an indication of pavement conditions per 50-meter section of road. This allows road sections to be analysed and compared to assist road maintenance teams in identifying areas that require maintenance. Overall, the purpose of the workflow is to provide a structured and repeatable process for detecting, classifying and mapping road defects using artificial intelligence, this improves efficiency, reduces manual inspection time, and supports data driven maintenance planning, and allows road authorities to prioritise inspections and repairs more effectively and efficiency

Overall, the project demonstrated the potential of developing a low-cost AI defect detection system using commonly found equipment such as mobile phones, phone mounts and a computer. Although the system was fully developed, further improvements are required before it could be reliably used for road inspections. Future work can include collecting a larger and more diverse dataset using a camera with a higher image quality. This is to be able to improve the model’s ability at generalization, detection accuracy and further developing the PDI calculation method. Aswell as developing the AI model additional testing across different types of road types, travelling speeds, environmental conditions would also help improve the reliability of the system for practical pavement maintenance application
