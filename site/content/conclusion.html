5.0 Conclusion

This project successfully developed and evaluated an AI-based road surface defect detection system to investigate its potential application for New Zealand pavement conditions. A baseline model was first trained using the RDD2022 dataset which included defects in an international setting. The labels for this dataset were then converted into YOLO text format for training and used to train multiple YOLO models. Then New Zealand-specific road defect footage was then collected and used to train the model on New Zealand pavement conditions.

The preliminary results showed that the model can learn pavement defects from the international dataset. Comparison of the small, large and extra-large YOLO models showed that increasing the model size led to improved performance up to a point. The large model achieved the best balance between performance and accuracy, compared with the small and extra-large models. However, when testing the extra-large model, the model did not improve compared to the large model. This may suggest that increasing the model size while keeping the image quality and the dataset the same may not mean that the model will improve. Therefore, the final model that is selected should be judged on a large New Zealand-specific dataset and real-world performance rather than performance using training results.

Following on from the preliminary results, further training was carried out to improve the YOLO model’s ability to detect pavement defects under New Zealand-specific pavement and environmental conditions. The final result showed that the model was able to identify different types of defects across different pavement and environmental conditions. However, the detection accuracy, reliability and consistency remained limitations. The final fine-tuning run achieved a peak mAP50 of 0.308, which was below the original project goal of 0.8. This indicates that further improvements are required before the model can achieve the desired detection performance.

During real-world testing, the AI would sometimes appear to perform better than the validation results suggested. However, this was based on visual observations rather than quantitative measurements, meaning that the actual detection performance could not be confirmed. The result highlighted the importance of using high-quality images and a large and varied dataset to achieve consistent defect detection across different road conditions.

In addition to the training of the YOLO model, a Python-based application and workflow were developed to allow users to upload road footage, automatically extract frames and GPS coordinates, run the YOLO model to detect and classify pavement defects, display the results on an interactive map, and create a small PDF file listing all the defects and their locations. The workflow was built to simplify the process of analysing road footage and reviewing detected defects. A simplified Pavement Distress Index (PDI) was also developed to provide an indication of pavement conditions per 50-metre section of road. This allows road sections to be analysed and compared to assist road maintenance teams in identifying areas that require maintenance. Overall, the purpose of the workflow is to provide a structured and repeatable process for detecting, classifying and mapping road defects using artificial intelligence. This improves efficiency, reduces manual inspection time, and supports data-driven maintenance planning, and allows road authorities to prioritise inspections and repairs more effectively and efficiently.

Overall, the project demonstrated the potential of using a low-cost AI defect detection system with commonly found equipment such as mobile phones, phone mounts and a computer. Although a working prototype was successfully developed, further improvements are required before it could be reliably used for road inspections. Future work can include collecting a larger and more diverse dataset using a camera with a higher image quality. This is to be able to improve the model’s generalisation ability, and detection accuracy, and to further develop the PDI calculation method. Alongside further development of the AI model, additional testing across different types of road types, travelling speeds and environmental conditions would also help improve the reliability of the system for practical pavement maintenance applications.
